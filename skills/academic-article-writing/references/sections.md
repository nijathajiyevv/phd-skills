# Sections: what each must do and how deep it must go

Apply to whatever sections the user's format has, via the function map. Section names below are generic.

## Section jobs

| Part | Job | Required moves | Typical failure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Title | Carry the central contribution | State the finding or object, not just the topic | Vague topic label, puns, acronyms |
| Abstract | Whole paper in miniature | Broad context, specific gap, "here we" approach, key results with numbers, answer to the gap, significance | Results before the gap; no numbers; promises instead of findings |
| Introduction | Make the reader care about one gap | CARS: establish territory, establish niche (gap), occupy niche (what this paper does); last paragraph previews findings and lists contributions where the field expects it | Literature dump; gap never named |
| Literature / related work / theory | Argue why this study is needed | Synthesis by theme, critique, derived gap, framework | Author-by-author summary |
| Methods | Allow replication and judgement of validity | See depth checklist below | Missing parameters; undeclared exclusions |
| Results | Prove the claim step by step | One question per paragraph, answered in its last sentence; declarative subheadings; figure titles state conclusions | Data dump in run order |
| Discussion | Show how the gap was filled and how far the claim reaches | Meaning of findings, comparison with prior work, limitations, implications, future work | Restating results; overclaiming |
| Conclusion | Close the loop | Answer to the question, contribution, one implication | New results or citations; hype |

Introduction pattern (operationalised CARS): field gap paragraph; subfield gap paragraph; paper gap paragraph with clues pointing to the hypothesis or method; final paragraph on what this paper does and finds.

Through-line test: extract the gap sentence from the introduction and the answer sentence from the discussion. If they do not match, flag a broken through-line before any line edit.

## Depth standards

### Literature: compare and contrast, never list

Build a synthesis matrix: themes as rows (or columns), sources on the other axis, with columns for method, sample, finding, limitation, and a synthesis column for the author's judgement. Method is an often overlooked point of divergence.

| Synthesis move | Paragraph does |
| --- | --- |
| Convergence | Several studies agree; state the finding once, cite all |
| Divergence | Studies disagree; explain why (method, sample, context, time) |
| Extension | Later work builds on earlier; show the progression |
| Critique | Shared weakness in a body of work |
| Gap | What none address, derived from the above |

Test: no paragraph is about a single source; each ends with what it means for this study.

### Methods: every detail a peer needs

- Design and rationale; setting; dates of data collection
- Participants or materials: inclusion and exclusion criteria, recruitment, sample size and its justification (power analysis or saturation or information power)
- Instruments: name, version, validity and reliability evidence, translation and adaptation
- Procedure in the order it happened; variables and operational definitions
- Data processing: cleaning, exclusions with counts, missing data handling, transformations
- Analysis: tests or models, assumptions checked, software and version, parameters, thresholds, multiple-comparison corrections
- Qualitative: researcher position, interview guide, transcription, coding approach, theme derivation, trustworthiness
- Ethics approval body and number, consent, preregistration, data and code availability, deviations from protocol

Test: could a competent peer repeat or audit the study from this section alone?

### Results: right content in the right format

| Message | Format |
| --- | --- |
| Fits in one or two sentences | Text only |
| Many exact values, precise comparisons | Table |
| Trend, pattern, relationship, distribution | Figure |
| Process, design, framework | Diagram |

Figure type follows the message: group comparison as dot or bar with error bars; change over time as line; distribution as histogram, box or violin; relationship as scatter; composition as stacked bar (avoid pie for more than a few parts). Never show the same data as both table and figure. Text states the takeaway and key number, not the whole table. Order by research question, primary before secondary. Every display is called out in text and stands alone with its caption.

### Discussion and conclusion: tie own data to the literature

For each main finding: the finding, comparison with studies reviewed earlier (agree or disagree, and why), explanation or mechanism, implication. Then limitations with the likely direction of bias, implications for theory and practice, specific future research. The conclusion answers the research question and states the contribution; no new data or citations.

Test: every major source from the introduction reappears in the discussion; every discussion claim traces to a result.
