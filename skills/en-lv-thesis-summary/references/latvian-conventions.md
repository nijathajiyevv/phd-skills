## 1. Register and syntax

Latvian academic prose is impersonal and nominal, but not agentless to the point of obscurity.

- Author self-reference: `promocijas darba autors` / `autore`, `darba autors secina`, or the impersonal `pētījumā konstatēts`. First person singular (`es uzskatu`) does not belong in a summary. `Autors uzskata` is standard; `mēs` is acceptable in some fields but choose one convention and hold it.
- Passive and reflexive constructions carry a lot of weight: `tika analizēts`, `tiek uzskatīts`, `analizēti dati`, `veikta aptauja`. Do not overuse `tika` where a plain past participle reads better.
- Debitive mood (`ir jāsecina`, `jāatzīmē`) is normal in Latvian academic writing and often the natural rendering of English "it should be noted that".
- Sentence length: Latvian tolerates long sentences, but English participial stacking ("Analyzing the collected data, having considered the limitations, the study finds...") must be broken into finite clauses. Latvian participles (`analizējot`, `ņemot vērā`) attach to the subject of the main clause . a dangling participle is a hard grammatical error in Latvian, not a style quibble.
- Word order is relatively free, so use it: place the informational focus at the end of the sentence rather than replicating English SVO order mechanically.

## 2. English-interference errors to eliminate

| English pattern | Wrong in Latvian | Correct |
|---|---|---|
| the/a articles | inserting `tas`, `viens` as articles | omit; Latvian has no articles |
| "in order to" | `priekš tā, lai` | `lai` + debitive/infinitive |
| "based on" | `bāzēts uz` | `balstoties uz`, `pamatojoties uz` |
| "in terms of" | `terminos par` | `attiecībā uz`, `no ... viedokļa` |
| "due to the fact that" | `sakarā ar to faktu, ka` | `tāpēc ka`, `jo` |
| possessive chain "the company's owner's decision" | three stacked genitives | restructure with prepositions or a relative clause |
| "impact on" | `iespaids uz` in academic register | `ietekme uz` |
| "significant" (statistical) | `nozīmīgs` used loosely | `statistiski nozīmīgs` when statistical, `būtisks` when substantive |
| "data is" | singular agreement | `dati` is plural: `dati liecina`, `dati tika apkopoti` |
| "research" as count noun | `pētījumi` where `pētniecība` is meant | distinguish `pētījums` (a study) from `pētniecība` (the activity) |
| "evidence" | `evidence` calque `evidences` | `pierādījumi`, `liecības`, `dati liecina` |
| "approach" | `pieeja` overused | `pieeja` is fine, but `metode`, `paņēmiens`, `skatījums` may fit better |
| "framework" | `ietvars` used for everything | `ietvarstruktūra`, `konceptuālais modelis`, `sistēma` depending on sense |
| "challenges" | `izaicinājumi` (originally a calque, now common) | acceptable, but `problēmas`, `grūtības`, `izaicinājumi` should be chosen by sense |

## 3. Case government traps

Latvian verbs and prepositions govern specific cases. Frequent errors in translated economics text:

- `ietekmēt` takes accusative: `ietekmē uzņēmumu konkurētspēju` (not `uz konkurētspēju`). But the noun `ietekme` takes `uz` + accusative: `ietekme uz konkurētspēju`.
- `sekot` takes dative: `sekot tendencēm`.
- `izmantot` takes accusative; `izmantošana` takes genitive: `datu izmantošana`.
- `atbilst` takes dative: `atbilst prasībām`.
- `veicināt` takes accusative: `veicina digitalizāciju`.
- `pamatoties` takes `uz` + accusative: `pamatojas uz teoriju`.
- After prepositions in plural, Latvian uses dative regardless of the singular government: `ar uzņēmumiem`, `par rezultātiem`, `no datiem`.
- Numerals: after `viens` singular nominative; after `divi` and above, plural; after quantity words like `daudz`, `vairāk` . genitive plural: `vairāk nekā 200 uzņēmumu` (not `uzņēmumi`).

## 4. Proper nouns and Latvianization

Latvian orthography adapts foreign proper nouns to Latvian phonology and adds grammatical endings. This is mandatory in running text, not optional.

**Personal names**: transcribe phonetically and add a Latvian ending, giving the original in parentheses at first mention.
- Kowalski → Kovaļskis (Kowalski)
- Nowak → Novaks (Nowak)
- Wiśniewski → Višņevskis (Wiśniewski)
- Female surnames take feminine endings: Kowalska → Kovaļska.
- In the bibliography, keep the original spelling unchanged. Latvianization applies to the narrative, not to reference entries.

**Place names**: use the established Latvian exonym where one exists.
- Warsaw / Warszawa → Varšava
- Kraków → Krakova
- Wrocław → Vroclava
- Poznań → Poznaņa
- Gdańsk → Gdaņska
- Łódź → Lodza
- Silesia / Śląsk → Silēzija
- Poland → Polija; Polish (adj.) → Polijas (of Poland) or poļu (ethnic/linguistic) . `Polijas MVU` for enterprises located in Poland, `poļu valoda` for the language

**Institutions and programmes**: give the Latvian translation of the name plus the original and, where used, the acronym at first mention; thereafter use the acronym.
- Główny Urząd Statystyczny (GUS) → Polijas Centrālā statistikas pārvalde (Główny Urząd Statystyczny, GUS)
- PARP → Polijas Uzņēmumu attīstības aģentūra (Polska Agencja Rozwoju Przedsiębiorczości, PARP)
- Eurostat, OECD, European Commission → Eurostat, ESAO (OECD), Eiropas Komisija. Note that OECD in Latvian is ESAO (Ekonomiskās sadarbības un attīstības organizācija); state both at first mention.

**EU legal acts and documents**: use the official Latvian title from EU sources; do not translate freshly. If the official Latvian title cannot be verified, give the English title and flag it in the query list.

## 5. Numbers, units, dates, currency

- Decimal separator is a comma: `3,14`; `0,05`. English `3.14` must be converted, and this conversion must never change the value.
- Thousands separator is a non-breaking space: `1 250 000`. Never a comma.
- Percent: space before the sign . `45 %`. Ranges: `20 %–30 %`.
- Statistical notation: `p < 0,05`; `R² = 0,74`; `n = 384`; `M = 3,42`; `SD = 0,87`. Latin abbreviations may be kept, but keep them consistent throughout.
- Dates: `2024. gada 15. martā` in running text; `15.03.2024.` in compact form. Note the ordinal periods . `2024. gadā`, not `2024 gadā`.
- Year ranges: `2015.–2023. gadā`.
- Currency: `EUR 1 500` or `1 500 EUR` . choose one and hold it. Polish złoty is `PLN`; in running text `zlots` / `zloti` is acceptable, and if converting to euro, state the rate and date, never convert silently.
- Units follow SI with a non-breaking space: `12 km`, `5 gadi`.

## 6. Punctuation, quotation marks, dashes

- Quotation marks are `„...”` (low opening, high closing). Nested: `„... «...» ...”`. Straight ASCII quotes are an orthographic error in a formal Latvian submission.
- Company and product names in Latvian text are often set in quotation marks: `uzņēmums „Nexus”`.
- Latvian uses the dash (domuzīme, `–`) with spaces, including as a copula replacing a missing verb: `Digitalizācija – uzņēmuma konkurētspējas priekšnosacījums`.
- Commas are obligatory before subordinate clauses and around participial constructions . Latvian comma rules are stricter and more formal than English ones. Every `kas`, `ka`, `lai`, `jo`, `kad`, `ja` clause boundary takes a comma.
- Enumerations in a list use `;` between items and `.` after the last when items are clauses.

## 8. Figures, tables, references

- Table caption goes **above** the table: `2. tabula. Aptaujāto uzņēmumu sadalījums pēc darbinieku skaita`.
- Figure caption goes **below** the figure: `3. attēls. Digitālā brieduma līmeņi Polijas MVU, 2023. gads`.
- Source line follows the caption: `Avots: autora veidots` / `Avots: autora aprēķini, izmantojot GUS datus` / `Avots: [Autors, gads]`.
- Every axis label, tick label, legend entry, series name and unit inside a chart is translated. A Latvian summary with an English x-axis is a visible defect.
- In-text cross-references: `sk. 3. att.`, `dati apkopoti 2. tab.`. Verify every cross-reference still points to the right numbered object after translation.
- **Bibliography stays in the original language.** Titles of English and Polish works are not translated. Only structural elements are Latvianized: `u.c.` for et al., and the section heading `Literatūras saraksts` / `Izmantotās literatūras un avotu saraksts`.
- If the receiving institution mandates a citation style, apply it; otherwise keep the source document's style and note the choice.

## 9. Orthography and diacritics

- The Latvian alphabet requires: ā, č, ē, ģ, ī, ķ, ļ, ņ, š, ū, ž. Omitting them changes words: `zale` (hall) vs `zāle` (grass); `kazas` (goat's) vs `kāzas` (wedding); `pile` (drop) vs `pīle` (duck).
- Long vowels in endings carry grammatical information . dropping a macron changes the case. This is the single highest-frequency defect in machine-produced Latvian.
- Soft consonants `ģ ķ ļ ņ` are distinct letters, not decorated variants.
- The letters `q, w, x, y` appear only in unadapted foreign material.
- Compound and hyphenation rules differ from English: `e-komercija`, `e-pārvalde`, `IKT nozare` (no hyphen), `MVU sektors`.
- Capitalization: Latvian capitalizes far less than English. Section headings take sentence case. Job titles, fields of study, and generic institution words are lowercase unless part of an official name. `promocijas darbs`, not `Promocijas Darbs`.
