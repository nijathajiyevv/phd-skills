# Thesis by publication and hybrid theses

Examined against the same standard as a monograph: examiners judge each paper and the coherence of the whole (Macquarie examiner guidelines).

| Aspect | Monograph | By publication or hybrid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Structure | Introduction, review, methodology, results, discussion, conclusion (or the discipline model) | General introduction; several papers, often four or more, published, accepted or submitted; linking text; integrative discussion; conclusion |
| Main risk | Late writing; drift from the question | Disconnected papers; repetition; unclear candidate contribution |
| Integration device | Chapter openings and closings | Linking sections before each paper |
| Authorship | Sole author | Contribution statement per co-authored paper |
| Examiner focus | Whole argument | Coherence and quality of the framing chapters, which are often unpublished and closely read |

## Do

- Choose the format early; design papers as parts of one question.
- Write an introduction stating the overarching question and how each paper answers part of it.
- Precede each paper with a linking section: publication status, the candidate's contribution, why it follows the previous paper.
- Write an integrative discussion that synthesises across papers, not a summary of each.
- Order papers by argument logic, not publication date.
- Check whether included papers keep journal formatting or must be reformatted, and whether references are per chapter or consolidated.

## Don't

- Staple papers together with a thin cover chapter; examiners flag papers only loosely linked to a coherent topic.
- Repeat the same methods, site descriptions and literature in every paper chapter without cross-referencing; excessive repetition is viewed as a weakness.
- Salami-slice one study into many small papers to reach a count.
- Assume publication protects a paper from examiner revision requests.

## Skill output: paper-to-question matrix

```
| Paper | Sub-question answered | Status | Candidate's role | Links to previous/next | Repetition to cut |
```

Then draft linking sections from the matrix, in the candidate's voice and within the candidate's chapter structure.
