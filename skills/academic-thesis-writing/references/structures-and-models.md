# Structures and discipline models

A catalogue for recognising the candidate's model and offering options when none is prescribed. It is a menu, never a default. Never "correct" a valid model into another one.

## Macrostructure types (Paltridge & Starfield)

| Type | Shape | Typical home |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Traditional simple | One study: introduction, review, method, results, discussion, conclusion | Many empirical master's and PhD theses |
| Traditional complex | General introduction and review; several studies each with method and results; general discussion | Sciences, psychology, engineering |
| Topic-based | Chapters organised by sub-topics of one argument | Humanities |
| Article compilation (manuscript style) | Introduction, papers, integrative discussion | Increasingly common in the sciences |
| Hybrid simple/manuscript | Traditional chapters plus article chapters | Education and other fields |
| Hybrid topic/manuscript | Topic chapters plus article chapters | Education and other fields |

Manuscript-style variants: retrospective (already published) versus prospective (written during candidature); sandwich (substantial introduction and conclusion around the papers) versus two-part (several chapters, then the papers).

## Discipline and regional models (examples)

| Model | Typical sequence | Examiner expectations |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Business and management, applied (common across many European programmes) | Introduction (aim, tasks, object, methods); theoretical part; analytical or practical part (organisation or market analysis, often with own research); project, suggestions and recommendations; conclusions | Theoretical part ends in a framework the analysis uses; recommendations follow from analysis findings, are justified, and shown to be appropriate and realistic; contribution stated in conclusions |
| Business and management, research-oriented | Introduction; theory and hypotheses; data and method; results; discussion with theoretical and practical relevance; recommendations and reflection | A theory chapter and an empirical part in every thesis |
| Business case-based | Theoretical chapter; company or case presentation (legal form, size, structure); case analysis; conclusions | Case described in detail; analysis tied to theory |
| Engineering and applied computing | Introduction; theoretical background; state of the art, often as a systematic review; research methodology; design or development; experiments and results; discussion; conclusion | A review chapter claiming to be systematic follows PRISMA reporting; requirements trace to evaluation; baselines |
| Empirical social sciences | Traditional simple or complex | Paradigm, design and analysis justified |
| Humanities | Topic-based argument | One thesis statement carried across chapters |
| Natural sciences | Traditional complex, compilation or hybrid | Integration across studies or papers |
| Professional doctorates (DBA, EdD) | Often traditional, shorter, linked to practice | Contribution to practice as well as knowledge |
| Practice-based arts | Creative work plus exegesis | Relationship between work and text |

## Research design skeletons (when the candidate asks how to structure)

| Design | Skeleton | What examiners probe |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quantitative | Introduction; review; hypotheses; method; results per hypothesis; discussion; conclusion | Measurement validity, sample size, statistics, causal claims |
| Qualitative | Introduction; review; methodology (paradigm); methods; findings by theme; discussion; conclusion | Paradigm fit, sampling logic, analytic transparency, reflexivity |
| Mixed methods | As above plus integration chapter or integrated discussion | Why mixing; where strands meet; contradictions handled |
| Review-based | Introduction; protocol and methods; search and screening results; synthesis; discussion | Search reproducibility, appraisal, contribution beyond summary |
| Design or engineering artefact | Problem; requirements; related work; design; implementation; evaluation; discussion | Evaluation rigour, baselines, generalisability |
| Theoretical or conceptual | Argument-driven chapters | Coherence, counter-positions, source handling |
| Case study | Case selection; context; data sources; within- and cross-case analysis | Selection logic, triangulation, analytic generalisation |

A descriptive summary of sources does not meet the standard at either degree level, even in a review-based thesis.

## Procedure

1. Recognise the model from the candidate's outline or regulations; state it back ("This follows the applied business model: theory, analysis, recommendations").
2. Load its expectations from the tables above and build the function map.
3. If nothing is prescribed, offer 2 or 3 fitting models with trade-offs.
4. Audit against the candidate's model, never against a generic template.
