# Degree standards and originality

## Master's versus PhD (Dublin descriptors)

| Dimension | Master's (second cycle) | Doctorate (third cycle) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Knowledge | Advanced knowledge giving a basis for originality, often in a research context | Systematic understanding of a field and mastery of its research methods |
| Research | Apply knowledge and problem-solving in new or unfamiliar settings | Conceive, design, implement and adapt a substantial research process with scholarly integrity |
| Contribution | Integrate knowledge, handle complexity, form judgements with incomplete information | Original research extending the frontier of knowledge; a substantial body of work, part of it worthy of refereed publication |
| Communication | Communicate conclusions and rationale clearly | Communicate with peers, the wider scholarly community and society |
| Length | Set by regulations; no universal standard | Set by regulations; monograph or papers plus framing chapters |

Calibration: "It's a PhD, not a Nobel Prize" (Mullins & Kiley 2002). Most doctoral contributions are incremental, and that is usually sufficient. A master's thesis needs competent, well-argued research and originality in developing or applying ideas, not a frontier-extending contribution. Do not inflate a master's project to PhD scope; do not treat a PhD as a big master's.

## Ways a thesis can be original (paraphrased from Phillips & Pugh, via Gill & Dolan 2015)

| Group | Forms |
| --- | --- |
| New material | New empirical work; first written account of significant new information; new evidence on an old question |
| New combination | Synthesis of things not combined before; cross-disciplinary approach or methods |
| New application | Known technique in a new area; something done elsewhere tried in a new country or setting; unexplored area within a discipline |
| New reading | Known material with a new interpretation; original test of someone else's theory |
| Continuation | Extending previously original work; one original technique, observation or result inside otherwise competent work |

## The core problem

Candidates often assert originality ("no one has done this before") instead of demonstrating what is now known that was not known before. If examiners cannot pinpoint the contribution, they may not award the degree. Under-readers mistake unfamiliarity with the literature for originality.

## Originality checks (proposal, mid-candidature, full draft, pre-submission)

1. **Placement:** the contribution appears in the abstract and in a dedicated section near the end stating what was known before and what is known now.
2. **Nearest neighbour:** even without a direct precedent, name the closest studies and the difference in question, context, method, data and finding.
3. **Demonstration:** flag every "first", "novel", "no one has" and require the supporting comparison.
4. **Breadth:** check the review is wide enough to justify the claim.
5. **Ownership:** in supervisor-designed or co-authored work, state the candidate's own contribution.
6. **Timing:** treat early contribution statements as provisional; finalise at the end.
7. **Consistency:** the same contribution wording in abstract, introduction, discussion and conclusion.

Output template:

```
Degree bar: <master's / PhD>
Contribution type(s): ...
Before: ... | After: ...
Nearest neighbours:
| Work | Question | Context/data | Method | Finding | Delta |
Assertions to evidence: <location> "<quote>" -> needs comparison with ...
Placement: abstract <yes/no>, dedicated section <yes/no/location>, conclusion <aligned/misaligned>
```

## Suggested edits

- Convert assertions into before/after statements with citations.
- Replace an exhaustive list of minor novelties with one focused contribution statement.
- Add the dedicated contribution section if missing, inside the candidate's existing conclusion chapter.
- Align wording across abstract, introduction, discussion and conclusion.

Never certify originality from memory; it rests on a documented search and named comparisons.
