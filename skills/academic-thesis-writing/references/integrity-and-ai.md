# Integrity and generative AI in theses

Thesis AI rules are institution-specific and tightening. Declarations are becoming mandatory; some universities return a thesis submitted without one (Université Laval). The Open University's 2025 guidance requires declarations at all stages of doctoral research and makes candidate, supervisor and examiner jointly responsible.

## Common pattern across 2025 university policies (always check the actual policy)

| Use | Usual status |
| --- | --- |
| Grammar, spelling, language polishing of the candidate's own text | Generally permitted; declare |
| Brainstorming, outlining, coding help, summarising sources the candidate has read | Often permitted with supervisor agreement; declare |
| Generating thesis text, arguments or interpretations | Generally prohibited or tightly restricted |
| Generating or altering data, images or results | Prohibited unless it is the research method, then described in the methodology |
| Uploading unpublished, participant or confidential data to public tools | Prohibited by data protection and ethics rules |
| Omitting or falsifying a required declaration | Misconduct everywhere |

## Skill behaviour

- Ask for the institution's thesis AI policy at session start.
- If generating thesis prose is prohibited or unknown, work in coaching mode: questions, critique, outlines, feedback on the candidate's own text, examples clearly labelled as illustrations. Explain why once.
- Encourage an AI-use log from the start (tool, version, date, purpose, what was kept).
- Draft the declaration on request, using the institution's template if provided:

```
In preparing this thesis I used [tool, version] for [purpose]. I reviewed and edited all output, and I take full responsibility for the content. The use complied with [institution] regulations and was agreed with my supervisor(s).
```

## Other integrity rules

- Verify every citation and factual claim an AI tool touched; LLMs fabricate plausible references at high rates.
- Reuse of the candidate's own published papers needs the citation and permissions the regulations require.
- No close paraphrase that mirrors the source's structure and wording.
- In co-authored or supervisor-designed work, state the candidate's own contribution.

## Refusals

Refuse to write submit-ready thesis sections where the policy prohibits it, to fabricate data or citations, to disguise AI-generated text, or to help omit a required declaration. Offer the legitimate alternative.
