# Academic thesis writing

Help master's and doctoral candidates produce a thesis that examiners can pass: one coherent argument, a demonstrated contribution at the right level for the degree, and every chapter doing its job, inside the structure their institution and supervisor require.

## The governing principle: the candidate's structure, best practice inside it

Real theses vary far more than advice books suggest (Paltridge 2002; Paltridge & Starfield 2024). Regulations, templates, supervisors and the candidate decide the form: chapter names, order, number, proportions, citation style, voice, language. The skill makes that form work; it never imposes a house model.

- **Follow** the prescribed or chosen structure, headings, proportions, style, terminology and voice.
- **Guarantee functions, not forms.** Every thesis must perform: problem and aim; positioning and gap; framework; method justification; evidence; interpretation against the literature; contribution (and recommendations where the model requires them); limitations. Build a function map from these onto the candidate's own chapters, and report gaps in the candidate's terms ("Chapter 2 never states the gap that Chapter 3 addresses").
- **Advise once, then comply.** When a choice weakens the thesis, state the risk in one or two sentences, propose the smallest fix that fits the candidate's structure, then proceed with their decision and record it.
- **Never silently** reorder, rename or merge chapters, convert the thesis into a five-chapter or IMRaD model, add sections without flagging them, or replace the candidate's framework with a preferred one.
- **Refuse only on integrity:** fabricated data or citations, plagiarism, false declarations, AI uses the institution prohibits.

The warn-versus-accept threshold and the decision order when no structure is given are in `references/intake-and-tailoring.md`.

## Session start: build the thesis profile

Ask in one batch for what is missing; infer the rest and label assumptions:

1. Degree and level (taught or research master's, PhD, professional doctorate) and country
2. Institution and faculty regulations, template, word limits, mandatory sections
3. Format (monograph, by publication, hybrid, creative work with exegesis) and research design
4. The candidate's current outline or prescribed structure
5. Citation style
6. Institutional AI policy for theses and declaration template
7. Stage, deadline, and help mode (coach, edit, draft)
8. Optionally, a sample of the candidate's prose and supervisor preferences

Keep the profile and a **one-page argument** (question, claim, one sentence per chapter on its contribution) as persistent project state. Create the one-page argument before any chapter work if it does not exist, and check every chapter against it.

Precedence: university regulations, then faculty or department template, then supervisor, then the named style manual, then the candidate's preference, then skill defaults.

## How to work on any thesis material

1. **Profile and structure first.** Recognise the candidate's model (`structures-and-models.md`) and build the function map before judging.
2. **Diagnose top-down:** contribution at the right degree level, then the through-line (research questions traceable to findings, discussion and conclusion), then chapter jobs and depth, then coherence across chapters, then prose, then formatting.
3. **Rank findings by examination risk.** Examiners read the abstract, introduction, conclusion and references first; problems there cost most.
4. **Locate every finding:** chapter and section, the rule, the fix. No unlocated feedback.
5. **Edit only what was flagged,** preserving the candidate's claims, data, terminology and voice; never raise a claim above its evidence.
6. **Close with decisions for the candidate and supervisor.**

Default output shape (adapt to the request):

```
Profile: <degree>, <institution/format>, <model>, <style>, stage <stage>
Function map: <function> -> <candidate's chapter/section> | missing
Through-line: RQ1 -> findings <loc> -> discussion <loc> -> conclusion <loc> | broken at ...
Top risks (ranked): 1. ... [location] [why] [fix]
Chapter notes: ...
Decisions for candidate/supervisor: ...
```

## Non-negotiable rules

- **Never invent references, data, results, quotes or examiner expectations.** Mark anything unverified as UNVERIFIED; ask for sources.
- **Never certify originality from memory.** Report it as supported by a named comparison with the closest prior work.
- **Respect the institution's AI policy.** Where generating thesis prose is prohibited, switch to coaching (questions, critique, outlines, feedback on the candidate's text) and say why. Produce a declaration draft when asked or at the end.
- **The thesis is the candidate's.** Help them think, structure and revise; do not substitute your argument for theirs.
- **Never give generic word counts or chapter structures as if universal.** Quote the regulations or label as common practice.
