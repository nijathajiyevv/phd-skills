# Intake and tailoring

## Thesis profile fields

| Field | Why it matters | Source of truth |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Degree and level | Master's (taught or research), PhD, professional doctorate: sets the originality bar | Programme handbook |
| Country and examination model | Viva, public defence, written-only; internal and external examiners | Graduate school regulations |
| Institution, faculty, department | Templates, mandatory sections, proportions | Regulations, template |
| Format | Monograph, by publication, hybrid, creative work plus exegesis | Regulations |
| Research design | Quantitative, qualitative, mixed, review-based, design or engineering, theoretical, case study | Proposal |
| Candidate's outline | The structure the skill must work within | Candidate, supervisor |
| Length and limits | Word cap and what counts toward it | Regulations |
| Citation style | Usually set by department or supervisor | Department guide |
| Language | Thesis language, US or UK variety, bilingual abstract requirements | Regulations |
| Supervisor preferences | Voice, chapter order, depth | Supervisor |
| AI policy for theses | Allowed uses, declaration template | Institutional policy |
| Writing sample | Voice matching | Candidate |
| Stage, deadline, help mode | Plan and mode of help | Candidate |

Precedence: university regulations, then faculty or department template, then supervisor, then style manual, then candidate preference, then skill default. When rules conflict, name the conflict and the rule followed.

## Decision order for structure

1. **Structure given** (regulations, template, supervisor or candidate): adopt exactly, including chapter names, order and page or word proportions. Example: a business programme prescribing theoretical basis, practical part, and suggestions and recommendations with page targets.
2. **Partly given:** keep what is given; propose missing parts as marked suggestions.
3. **Nothing given:** offer 2 or 3 models that fit discipline and design (`structures-and-models.md`), each with a one-line trade-off; the candidate chooses, ideally after checking with the supervisor.

## Function map

| Function | Question | Possible homes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Problem and aim | What, why, for whom | Introduction; "aim, tasks, object and subject" section |
| Positioning and gap | What is known and missing | Literature review; theoretical part; state-of-the-art or systematic review chapter |
| Framework | Which concepts or model guide the study | End of theoretical part; separate framework chapter |
| Method justification | How and why this way | Methodology chapter; methods inside the analytical part; per-paper methods |
| Evidence | What was found | Results; analytical or empirical part; paper chapters |
| Interpretation | Meaning against the literature | Discussion; integrative chapter; within findings chapters |
| Contribution and application | What is new; what should be done | Conclusions; project or recommendations part; contribution section |
| Limitations | How far it holds | Methodology; discussion; conclusion |

Output the map as a table with the candidate's chapter numbers and titles.

## Warn versus accept: the deviation threshold

Warn once, then comply:

- A function is missing anywhere in the thesis (no gap, no framework where the model expects one, no method justification, no limitations, no contribution statement)
- Research questions not answered, or answered only partly, in the conclusion
- Recommendations or practical proposals not derived from the analysis (common in applied business theses)
- Claims exceed evidence or degree-level scope
- Structure conflicts with a regulation (missing mandatory section, over the word limit)
- A review chapter called systematic without systematic methods
- By-publication: papers not linked to one question; candidate contribution to co-authored papers not stated

Accept silently as preference:

- Chapter names, numbering and order that still cover the functions
- Discipline models that differ from the five-chapter model
- Proportions set by regulations or supervisor
- Voice and first person where discipline and supervisor accept them
- Terminology used consistently

Record each warned decision and the candidate's answer; do not reopen it unless asked.

## Voice preservation

A thesis must read as one person's work across years. Edits fix identified problems; they do not homogenise style. For non-native writers, prioritise correctness and consistency: examiners tolerate idiosyncratic grammar more than sloppiness.
