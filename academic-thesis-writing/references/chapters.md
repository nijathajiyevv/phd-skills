# Chapter jobs and depth

Chapter names here are generic. Apply them through the function map to the candidate's own chapters (for example, a business "theoretical part" carries the literature review and framework jobs; an engineering "state of the art" chapter carries the review job).

Examiners usually read the abstract, introduction and conclusion first, then the references, before the middle. These set the verdict frame.

## Jobs

| Chapter role | Job | Must contain | Typical failure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Abstract | Whole thesis in brief | Problem, gap, question, approach, main findings, contribution, implication | Lists chapters instead of findings |
| Introduction | Frame the argument | Context, problem, gap, research questions (or aim and tasks), contribution claim, scope, chapter map | Written first and never revised; promises the conclusion does not keep |
| Literature review / theoretical part / state of the art | Argue why the study is needed | Thematic synthesis, critique, derived gap, framework | Author-by-author summary; exhaustive and argument-free |
| Methodology | Justify how the question is answered | Rationale, design, sampling, instruments, procedure, analysis, validity or trustworthiness, ethics, design limitations | Describes without justifying against alternatives |
| Results / findings / analytical part | Present evidence per question | Organised by research question, displays that state conclusions | Every output dumped; interpretation mixed in unsignposted |
| Discussion | Interpret against the literature | Answer per question, comparison with reviewed studies, explanation, implications, limitations | Restates results; no link back to the review |
| Recommendations or project part (applied models) | Turn findings into proposals | Each proposal traced to a finding, justified, with expected effect and feasibility | Generic advice unrelated to the analysis |
| Conclusion | Close the argument | Answer per question, dedicated contribution statement, implications, specific future research | New data; "more research is needed" |

Bookends: every question raised in the introduction is answered in the conclusion in the same terms. Rewrite both last.

## Depth standards

### Literature review: compare, contrast, critique

Build a synthesis matrix: themes against sources, with columns for method, sample, finding, limitation, and a synthesis column for the candidate's judgement. Never build the review from source-by-source summaries. Each section runs: theme claim, convergent evidence, divergent evidence with why studies differ, critique of the body of work, implication for this thesis. The chapter ends in the gap and the framework that the methodology operationalises. Boote and Beile argue that doctoral reviews are often misconceived as exhaustive summary rather than analysis and synthesis that justify the study.

### Methodology: every detail, every justification

Everything an article needs (design, setting, dates, sample and its justification, instruments with versions and validity evidence, procedure, variable definitions, data processing and exclusions, analysis with software and parameters, ethics approval, data availability), plus:

- Paradigmatic position where the field expects it
- Justification of each major choice against named alternatives
- Pilot work and what changed
- Full instruments, protocols and codebooks in appendices
- Design limitations stated here, not only in the discussion

Test: an examiner can replicate or audit every step and see why each was chosen.

### Results or findings: right format, organised by question

- Restate the question at the start of each results chapter or section.
- Choose format by message: one or two sentences as text; exact values as tables; trends, patterns and relationships as figures; processes and frameworks as diagrams.
- Never present the same data twice; full tables to appendices, key ones in the chapter.
- Qualitative findings: themes defined, prevalence language careful, quotes as evidence not decoration.
- End each results chapter with a short summary of what it established.

### Discussion and conclusion: own data in dialogue with the literature

- For each research question: answer, comparison with studies from the review, explanation of agreement or difference, implication.
- Address unexpected findings directly; new literature is allowed when a finding needs it.
- Triangulate across studies or strands.
- Limitations with their likely effect on conclusions.
- Conclusion: answer per question, dedicated contribution statement, specific future research, no new data.

Test: every key source from the review returns in the discussion; every discussion claim traces back to a result.
