# Writing craft, register and presentation balance

The author is the worst judge of clarity because they already know the content. These rules simulate the naive reader. Readability of scientific abstracts has declined steadily (709,577 abstracts, 1881 to 2015), tracking growing general scientific jargon rather than necessary technical terms (Plavén-Sigray et al., eLife 2017). Aim for precise and plain at once.

## Craft rules

1. Outline one informal sentence per planned paragraph before drafting; fix structure at outline stage.
2. Paragraphs run context, content, conclusion: first sentence states the topic, last sentence the takeaway. Skimming first and last sentences must give the argument.
3. Old information at the start of a sentence, new or emphasised information at the end; keep subject and verb close (Gopen & Swan 1990).
4. One term per concept everywhere. Synonyms make readers suspect a different meaning.
5. Parallel syntax for parallel points; keep related ideas adjacent; touch each subject in one place (no zig-zag).
6. Define every technical term at first use; minimise acronyms.
7. Report effect sizes, uncertainty and sample sizes with every numerical claim.
8. Hedge to the exact strength of the evidence.
9. Rewrite stuck paragraphs from scratch rather than patching.

## Register

| Keep | Cut or replace |
| --- | --- |
| Field-specific technical terms, defined | Tone jargon: "utilise", "facilitate", "leverage", "novel", "robust" without evidence |
| Hedges matched to evidence ("suggests", "is associated with") | Stacked hedges ("may possibly suggest"); unearned boosters ("clearly proves") |
| Formal, direct sentences, mostly one idea each | Colloquialisms, contractions, rhetorical questions (in most fields) |
| "We" where the venue allows | Passive voice hiding who did what when it matters |
| Signposting at section and paragraph starts | Throat-clearing ("It is important to note that") |

Hedges and boosters tune claim strength; hedges cluster in discussion sections. Calibration varies by discipline and language background, so compare against the venue's recent papers, not a fixed ratio.

## Visual balance

- Each key result has exactly one home: text, table or figure.
- Main-text displays carry the central argument; full detail goes to supplementary material.
- Each display stands alone: caption explains what, sample or source, abbreviations, statistics.
- Consistent abbreviations, group order, colours and notation across displays.
- Avoid 3D charts, rainbow colour maps, truncated axes that exaggerate effects.

## Logical balance

Each analytical paragraph carries claim, evidence and the reasoning linking them. Flag descriptive paragraphs without a claim and claims without evidence.

## Procedure

Run two separate passes, never merged:

1. **Clarity pass:** reverse outline (one line per paragraph), term consistency, definitions, sentence-level fixes, jargon list.
2. **Register pass:** hedging calibration against evidence, formality, venue match, voice preservation.

Readability scores are signals, never targets. Preserve the author's voice; non-native writers need correctness and consistency, not native-sounding prose.
